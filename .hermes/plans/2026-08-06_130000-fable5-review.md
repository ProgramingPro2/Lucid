## 0. TL;DR

1. **The corpus is not what the whole program assumes it is.** `data/corpus_1m.txt` is WikiText-style Wikipedia (the ` @-@ ` / ` @,@ ` escapes are visible in the raw text) mixed with Project Gutenberg fiction, pre-flattened into ~113,215 blank-line-separated blocks with **no article/section structure surviving** (zero heading lines in the file). `load_documents` treats each block as a "document," so a "document" is a single real-world paragraph (~1.1 KB, ~8–14 sentences). The "paragraph-scale reasoner" is therefore learning *first-half-of-a-paragraph → second-half-of-the-same-paragraph*, not discourse. This caps what any downstream model can learn, and neither plan flags it.

2. **The fixes plan's Step A (stride=1 scale-up), as specified, silently converts the prediction task into near-copy detection.** With overlapping G=5 windows at stride 1, the target window shares 4 of 5 sentences with the last context window. M3 would shoot up for a fake reason and the identity control would be the only thing standing between you and a week-long mirage. This is the single most dangerous unflagged item in the current roadmap.

3. **`src/conceptpoc/predictor/diffusion.py` is not "already correct."** Both plans designate it read-only. It has a first-order design flaw for this application (unit-norm 512-d targets against unit-variance noise ⇒ per-dim SNR ≥ 1 for only ~4 of 100 timesteps ⇒ the denoiser is trained overwhelmingly as a **conditional-mean regressor** — the exact mean-seeking failure Strategy B was chosen to escape), a non-zero terminal SNR (ᾱ_T ≈ 0.36, so sampling starts out-of-distribution), a DDPM branch that is mathematically wrong and would crash on shape, and a CFG-on-x0 interaction the plan's recommended config (`predict_type="x0"`, `cfg_scale=3.0`) makes worse.

4. **The writer decodes the wrong map.** Phase-2 trains `writer(concept(sentence i−1)) → tokens(sentence i)` — a *next-sentence-from-previous-concept* model — while `render_paragraph` and every plan treat it as "express this concept." It is also trained on pairs that cross document boundaries, with a hardcoded wrong pad id, without ever seeing the BOS token it is primed with at inference, and with FiLM frozen (zero-initialized) in 9 of 12 blocks. "The writer is confirmed generative and conditioned" is true, but it is conditioned on the wrong thing, and every renderability number and Step E's oracle A/B inherits this.

5. **Strategic verdict (expanded in Part 5): the direction "model p(x|context) and sample" is right; the specific compute plan is premature.** Three structural problems (corpus structure, writer semantics, diffusion scaling) must be fixed first or Step D measures a mean-seeking sampler over intra-paragraph cohesion rendered by a mis-mapped writer. There is also a missing cheap rung on the generative-model ladder (a conditional vMF/mixture-density head) that should be the week-1 baseline before any diffusion run.

## 2. Part 1 — Oversights: new findings on top of the existing plans

### A. The corpus (nobody has looked at the data)

**A1. "Documents" are single paragraphs; article structure was destroyed upstream.**
`data/corpus_1m.txt` contains 113,215 blank-line-separated blocks averaging ~1.1 KB. Sample blocks are recognizably WikiText-103-style Wikipedia (spaced punctuation, ` @-@ ` hyphen escapes, ` @,@ ` number escapes) interleaved with Gutenberg fiction (Sherlock Holmes dialogue, Plato's Republic). A grep for WikiText heading lines (`= ... =`) returns zero matches — the section/article markers were stripped when this file was made, so consecutive paragraphs of the same article are unlinked, and `_split_blocks` (`src/conceptpoc/data/corpus.py:47-50`) makes each paragraph an independent "document."

Consequences:
- The G=5 "paragraph concepts" are *half-paragraphs* of a real paragraph. The measured paragraph-scale predictability (M3 36–47%, mirage caveats aside) is **intra-paragraph topical cohesion**, not document-level structure. There is no data from which any reasoner could learn how a *thought develops across paragraphs* — the phenomenon the project is actually after.
- The fixes plan's Step A success gate "median paragraphs-per-document ≥ 4" is **unreachable in the meaningful sense**: most blocks have 8–14 sentences ⇒ 2–3 non-overlapping G=5 groups, forever. Stride-1 inflates the count with 80%-overlapping duplicates (see B1), which is not the same thing.
- Register mix (encyclopedic + Victorian fiction + philosophy) is a strong latent confound: a conditional model can score well on "coherence" by mere register-matching, and "novelty" by register-blending.

*Fix:* rebuild the corpus from a source with real document boundaries — raw WikiText-103 (headings intact ⇒ articles of 10–100 paragraphs) and/or Gutenberg with chapter segmentation. This costs one preprocessing script plus the already-planned encoding pass, and it converts the project's central question from unanswerable to answerable. It should happen **before** the reference distribution `R` is frozen, because it changes every downstream artifact once.

**A2. WikiText escape artifacts pollute the writer and every text metric.** The writer (both phases) is trained on text containing ` @-@ `, ` @,@ `, and spaced punctuation; it will generate them, and 5-gram-overlap/ROUGE novelty metrics will be distorted by them. A trivial detokenization pass (`@-@`→`-`, `@,@`→`,`, collapse space-before-punct) is missing.

**A3. The sentence segmenter silently breaks adjacency — the project's core quantity.** `segment_sentences` (`src/conceptpoc/data/segment.py:28-56`) drops sentences shorter than 12 chars and splits on patterns like `H. W. Bush` (period + space + capital), producing fragments that then get dropped by `min_chars`. Every "adjacent pair" dataset in the repo therefore contains pairs that are not adjacent in the source text, and truncation at 400 chars cuts sentences mid-way. For a research program whose base measurement is "how predictable is the adjacent unit," noise injected at this layer is unaccounted for in every measured number.

### B. A task-destroying interaction inside the fixes plan itself

**B1. Stride-1 grouping + the existing dataset definition = near-copy prediction.**
The fixes plan Step A says: change `_group_doc_sentences` to a sliding window with `stride=1` and train on the result. But `ParagraphSequenceDataset` (`experiments/paragraph_concepts/data.py:324-352`) defines the target as the *next index* in the paragraph array, and the context as the windows immediately before it. With stride-1 windows, **the target shares G−1 = 4 of 5 sentences with the last context element**. `cos(target, last_context)` ≈ 0.95+, the task becomes "predict a small perturbation of what you were just given," M3 top-1 will leap to a spectacular number, and only the identity-baseline control (Step B) would catch it after the fact.

Secondary effects nobody priced in:
- `n_following_pos=2` positives become 60–80%-overlapping copies of the target — InfoNCE trains toward near-duplicates.
- In-document negatives at stride 1 include windows overlapping the target (e.g., offset ±1), i.e., **near-positives labeled as negatives**: the same content receives positive and negative gradients simultaneously.
- The "real adjacent-paragraph cosine band" (Step B's coherence gate) becomes ~0.95 and meaningless.
- The strategy doc's memorization argument ("≥100k windows ⇒ a 5M denoiser won't memorize") overstates the effective sample size: stride-1 windows are not independent samples; effective n stays ≈ blocks × (sents−G+1)/G-ish in information terms.

*Fix (specific):* stride-1 is fine for **densifying the bank**, but training/eval pairs must enforce a positional margin: target index ≥ last-context index + G (disjoint windows), negatives and eval distractors excluded within |Δposition| < G of the target, and adjacency bands computed on disjoint pairs. ~15 lines in `ParagraphSequenceDataset.__getitem__` and `evaluate_m3`, but it must be in the spec **before** Step A runs, not discovered by the identity control after.

### C. The diffusion engine (both plans mark `diffusion.py` "read-only / already correct" — it is neither)

**C1. Norm-scale mismatch reintroduces the mean-seeking failure Strategy B exists to avoid.**
`diffusion_loss` normalizes targets to the unit sphere (`diffusion.py:248`: `x0 = F.normalize(target, dim=-1)`) and adds standard Gaussian noise (`:253`). A unit-norm 512-d vector has per-dimension std 1/√512 ≈ 0.044; the noise has per-dimension std 1.0 — the noise is ~22.6× larger in norm than the signal. With the schedule as configured (linear β 1e-4→0.02, T=100; `config.py:58-60`), per-dimension amplitude SNR √(ᾱ/(1−ᾱ))·(1/√512) exceeds 1 for only roughly the **first 4 of 100 timesteps**; it is ~0.2 at t=20 and ~0.08 at t=50. Under uniform timestep sampling (`:251`), ≥95% of training gradients ask the head to predict x0 from an input in which x0 is invisible — and the MSE-optimal answer there is **E[x0 | context], the conditional mean**. The head is thus trained overwhelmingly as a conditional-mean regressor with weak dependence on x_t; at sampling time the DDIM trajectory correspondingly collapses toward `mean + small noise admixture`. That is Strategy C (perturbed point prediction) wearing Strategy B's clothes. Consistent supporting evidence: the sentence-level diffusion predictor already trained in this repo (`configs/jepa_v3_128t.json`, `predictor_diffusion.pt`) "did not break the wall" (brainstorm §C6), and its rollouts sit far off-manifold (`eval_hybrid_film/report.txt`: raw `bank=0.365`).
*Fix:* scale targets to per-dim unit variance — `x0 ← x0·√dim` in `diffusion_loss` and divide by √dim before the final normalize in `sample()` (≈5 lines) — exactly the latent-scaling every latent-diffusion system does. Optionally add min-SNR-γ timestep weighting. **This must land before Step D or Step D's result is uninterpretable.**

**C2. Non-zero terminal SNR: sampling starts out-of-distribution.** With Σβ ≈ 1.005, ᾱ_T ≈ 0.36, so q(x_T|x0) = N(0.60·x0, 0.63·I), while `sample()` initializes from N(0, I) (`diffusion.py:312`). Wrong scale (E‖x_T‖ ≈ 18.1 vs 22.6) and the model never trained at true pure noise. This is the well-documented "flawed schedules" defect (Lin et al. 2023). Fix alongside C1: zero-terminal-SNR rescale of ᾱ, or a cosine schedule; consider v-prediction while touching it.

**C3. The DDPM sampler branch is wrong twice and would crash.** `diffusion.py:343-363`: the reverse step uses `x = x0 + √var·noise` — the posterior mean of DDPM is `c1·x̂0 + c2·x_t`, not `x̂0`; and `var = posterior_variance[t]` has shape (B,), which cannot broadcast against `noise` (B, 512) — a runtime shape error for any batch ≠ 512. Dead code today (default sampler is DDIM) but a landmine for the sweep. Fix or delete + assert `sampler == "ddim"`.

**C4. The plan's recommended config combines x0-parameterization with CFG scale 3.0 — an unflagged interaction.** `sample()` extrapolates model outputs linearly: `out = out_uncond + 3.0·(out_cond − out_uncond)` (`diffusion.py:333-335`). In x0-space this produces vectors far off the sphere shell, and `ddim_step` then back-derives ε from that extrapolated x0, amplifying it further. CFG for x0-prediction should be applied in ε-space (convert → guide → convert back) or the scale kept ≈1.5–2. The fixes plan D1 flags the `use_film`/`cfg_drop_prob` traps but not this one, and it recommends exactly the risky combination.

**C5. The final DDIM step is a no-op.** `torch.linspace(T−1, 0, steps)` ends at t=0 with t_prev=0 ⇒ `ddim_step` returns x unchanged; one of 30 steps (two model evals under CFG) is wasted. Cosmetic, but free to fix.

### D. The writer stack decodes the wrong map (largest single blind spot in both plans)

**D1. Phase-2 semantic off-by-one.** `ConceptSentenceDataset` (`src/conceptpoc/writer/train_phase2.py:41-45`):
```python
for i in range(1, len(sents)):
    ctx = all_vecs[i - 1]   # concept of the PREVIOUS sentence
    target = sents[i]       # tokens of the CURRENT sentence
```
The deployed writer is a *next-sentence-given-previous-sentence-concept* model. But `render_paragraph` (`experiments/paragraph_concepts/generate.py:72-106`) and both plans use it as "express concept c." So the pipeline's decode step actually performs one extra implicit prediction step, and:
- every renderability metric (`cos(re-encode(render(v)), v)`) measures topic-persistence-under-one-extra-hop, not expressibility;
- the reasoner→writer composition double-steps (reasoner predicts next paragraph, writer renders the sentence *after* its conditioning);
- Step E's "oracle" arm (render the paragraph's real constituent sentence vectors) does not measure what the plan thinks it does — it renders each sentence's *successor*.
*Fix:* retrain Phase-2 with same-sentence pairing (`ctx = all_vecs[i]`), which is a one-line data change plus the D2–D4 fixes below, ~2–4 GPU-hours at the config in `configs/writer_phase2.json`. Keep the old checkpoint for A/B. The fixes plan's global "do not touch the writer" freeze locks this defect in.

**D2. Phase-2 pairs cross document boundaries.** `sents = [s for d in docs for s in d.sentences]` (`train_phase2.py:31`) flattens all documents before pairing; with ~10-sentence blocks, ~10% of training pairs condition the writer on an unrelated document's last sentence — directly training "sometimes ignore the concept." One-line fix (pair within documents).

**D3. Wrong pad id + BOS never trained.** `_collate` hardcodes `pad_id = 0` and masks `labels == 0` (`train_phase2.py:59,160`), but the GPT-2 tokenizer's added `[PAD]` is id ~50257 (`src/conceptpoc/jepa/tokenizer.py:44-53`). Token 0 is `!`: inputs are padded with `!`, and every genuine `!` in the corpus is masked out of the loss. It works only by accident. Separately, neither phase ever includes BOS in inputs (`train_phase2.py:162` feeds `tgt[:, :-1]` with no BOS; Phase-1 windows have none), yet `FluWriter.generate` primes with `bos_id` (`writer/model.py:203`) — the first generated token is conditioned on an embedding never seen in training, and Phase-2 never trains prediction of a sentence's first token at all.

**D4. FiLM is dead in 9 of 12 blocks of the deployed writer.** Phase-2 freezes everything except `concept_proj` and the top 3 blocks (`train_phase2.py:107-110`); FiLM projections are zero-initialized (`writer/model.py:100-104`), so blocks 0–8 have FiLM = identity, frozen. The "concept re-injected at every layer" story the plans repeat is 75% inactive in the artifact. Cheap high-value change: include all `blocks.*.film` in the trainable list (they are tiny) while keeping attention/FFN frozen.

**D5. Generation-loop mechanics.** `generate()` has no KV cache (full re-forward per token, `writer/model.py:205-233`) — at Step D's evaluation scale (1,000 contexts × 8 samples × 5 sentences × ~96 tokens through a 152M model) this dominates wall-clock; and the repetition penalty pools `seen` tokens **across the whole batch** (`:207-214`), correct only for batch=1 (current usage — but it silently breaks the moment anyone batches renders for the eval suite).

### E. Reasoner and evaluation — beyond the fixes plan's Findings 1–3

**E1. Best-checkpoint selection is a winner's curse on resampled evals.** `evaluate_m3(..., seed=seed + epoch)` (`experiments/paragraph_concepts/train.py:256`) redraws the distractor sets every epoch, and best-epoch selection takes the max over 10 such draws at n=200 (`:261-264`). The headline 36% is the maximum of ten noisy estimates of a quantity whose per-draw σ ≈ 3.3 points (val sequence 0.335/0.36/0.33/… is consistent with a flat ~0.33 + noise). The fixes plan computed the CI but not the selection bias, which additionally means the "re-measured honest baseline" must fix the eval seed per run, not only enlarge n.

**E2. "Top-1 of 100" has a fake denominator; MRR is worse.** Distractors are drawn **with replacement** from the in-doc pool (`train.py:84-88`), so with pool sizes of 1–5, top-1 is really top-1-of-(1+|pool|), and MRR counts duplicated distractors as separate rank slots. Report the distinct-candidate count with every M3 number; treat historical MRR as unusable.

**E3. The multi-positive objective trains a different target than the metric measures.** With `n_following_pos=2`, `ranking_loss` (`reasoner.py:117-145`, `lse_all − lse_pos`) is optimized by a key aligned to the *centroid of the next three paragraphs* — a horizon-averaged target — while M3 scores exact-next. This is not just the eval-contamination Opus found; training actively optimizes a smeared objective. Decide which task it is: exact-next (set `n_following_pos=0`) or horizon (score any-of-k). Run both arms; the fixes plan exposes the flag but frames it only as a contradiction to "resolve."

**E4. The reasoner's key lives in a ≤256-dim affine subspace and is unconstrained off the candidate span — the strategy doc's "the key is already a novel point" claim is geometrically unsupported.** `out_proj` is `Linear(d_model=256 → 512)` (`reasoner.py:73`): every key lies in a fixed ≤257-dim affine slice of concept space, which the bank does not occupy. Moreover the only training signal is relative cosine against ≤|doc| in-document candidates per example; nothing anchors the key to the paragraph manifold globally (no global negatives, no norm/manifold regularizer). Two implications: (i) the strategy doc §2.1's framing that "only `retrieve_nearest` destroys the novelty" of an already-novel key is wrong — raw keys are systematically off-manifold in a *structured* way; (ii) probe C1 (σ-sweep around the key) explores the neighborhood of a point that was never trained to be a concept; add an arm that perturbs around the *top-1 retrieved bank vector* to separate "manifold is thin" from "key is off-manifold."

### F. Smaller but real

**F1. `TrajectoryPlanner` is architecturally hollow (relevant because "the planner underdelivered" is part of the measured history).** `planner.py:37-41`: `layer = nn.TransformerEncoderLayer(...); self.latent_blocks = nn.ModuleList([layer for _ in range(n_layers)])` registers the **same layer object four times** — "4 layers" is one layer's weights applied 4×. And the "transformer refinement" runs on a sequence of length 1 (`h.unsqueeze(1)`), where self-attention is a per-token linear map — the refinement stack is an MLP in disguise. Any conclusions drawn from planner experiments are confounded.

**F2. `latent_diagnostics` reports raw, uncentered cosines** (`src/conceptpoc/eval/latent.py`), inconsistent with the centered+anisotropy-floor methodology in `eval/metrics.py` that caught previous mirages. The `bank=0.365` in `eval_hybrid_film/report.txt` is unanchored. The new evalsuite fixes this going forward; also patch the harness or stop quoting its numbers.

**F3. Cache pick by lexicographic sort.** `run_samples` selects `sorted(cache_dir.glob(...))[-1]` (`generate.py:198-201`) — "latest" by hash-string order, i.e., arbitrary once a second cache exists (which Step A creates). Pin exact cache paths.

**F4. `_cache_key` ignores `keep_sent_vectors` even today** (`data.py:120-131`): building with `keep_sent_vectors=True` after a False-cache exists silently loads the cache without sentence vectors. (The fixes plan catches stride/min_sents for Step A; note this one bites *now*, before any new parameters exist.)

**F5. `evaluate_m3` context-ban arithmetic is correct but brittle** (`train.py:77-79` reconstructs `ctx_start_local` from the tensor shape); fine today, will break silently if context construction changes. Assert instead of re-deriving.

## 3. Part 2 — Architectural reframes not in the plans

### 3.1 Is the single pooled vector the right interface? Three separable answers

The system uses one 512-d mean-pooled, L2-normalized vector at every joint: sentence→concept (`encoder.py:47-50`), 5 sentences→paragraph (`data.py:146-148`), reasoner key, diffusion target, writer conditioning. Three distinct problems hide in that uniformity:

- **For the reasoner's *input*, pooling is pure loss.** The sentence vectors already exist (and Step A persists them). Feeding the reasoner the *sequence of sentence vectors* (5× longer context, same information source) while keeping the *paragraph centroid as the prediction target* is a strictly-more-information change that requires only a different dataloader view — no new encoders, no new interfaces. The brainstorm's D1 (token-level cross-attention) was rejected as heavy; this sentence-level middle option costs almost nothing and was never listed.
- **For the *target/bank*, the centroid is defensible** (the convex-hull geometry argument in strategy doc §2.2 is real), but note it inherits a badly damaged sentence geometry: with adjacent-sentence cosine ≈ 0.05 by construction (the encoder's trained-in repulsion), a 5-sentence centroid retains ~1/√5 of each sentence's signal and lives in whatever tiny shared subspace survived the uniformity penalty. Paragraph concepts are *low-information projections of an anti-correlated space*. This is why encoder repair (below) belongs on the critical path, not the fallback branch.
- **For the *writer conditioning*, one vector per sentence is fine — the problem is which vector** (Part 1 D1) and that the writer gets no textual context at all (see 3.2).
- **Grouping by fixed G cuts mid-thought.** In this corpus, blocks *are* natural paragraphs; after the corpus rebuild (A1) the natural unit is the real paragraph (variable length) with the article as the document. Fixed-G was a workaround for not having structure; once structure exists, drop it (mean-pool over the actual paragraph; the writer's `n_sentences` follows the source).

### 3.2 Two-channel conditioning: stop making the concept vector do local coherence

The measured facts say: sentence-level forward predictability in concept space ≈ 0; paragraph/topic-level predictability is real; the writer's token LM is the only component that demonstrably produces fluent language. The architecture that matches those facts conditions the writer on **both** the previous sentences' *text* (a token-level prefix — local coherence, entity carry-over, style) **and** the concept vector via FiLM (the *steering arc* — where the paragraph is going). The brainstorm called this D5/G3 and worried it "abandons the thesis"; that worry is misplaced given the goal stated for this review — *novel coherent multi-sentence text* — because in the two-channel design the concept channel is exactly where sampled novelty enters, and it is relieved of the job (local fluency glue) it measurably cannot do. The concept-ablation control (shuffle the concept, verify output changes) keeps the thesis honest. This is a Phase-2.5 writer fine-tune: same data, prepend the previous sentence's tokens to the input, keep FiLM. It also fixes the D1 off-by-one *conceptually*: with textual context present, the concept can be the current target's concept without ambiguity.

### 3.3 The missing rung on the generative ladder: cheap conditional densities before diffusion

The plans jump from "point regression failed" to "conditional diffusion." Between them sit two standard, near-free conditional densities, either of which is a *day* of work on the existing trunk and either of which might win outright at this data scale:

- **Conditional von Mises–Fisher head:** predict mean direction μ(c) and concentration κ(c); closed-form log-likelihood on the sphere; sampling is exact and cheap; κ is a *learned, context-dependent* coherence/novelty dial (precisely Strategy C's "learned trust radius," done on the correct manifold instead of with post-hoc Gaussian jitter). ~60 lines.
- **Mixture density network (K≈8–32 vMF or low-rank Gaussian components):** the minimal model that *represents multimodality explicitly* — mixture weights per context directly answer "is the next-paragraph distribution actually multimodal?", which is the empirical premise of the whole Strategy B argument and is currently untested. ~120 lines.

These share the eval harness with Strategy B and become the generative baselines the diffusion model must beat. If a 60-line vMF head matches the diffusion prior on the novelty/coherence frontier, that is a decisive architectural result at 1% of the cost. Not listing any of this family is the strategy doc's biggest menu omission (its D — full sequential VAE — is the heavyweight cousin, correctly deprioritized; the non-sequential heads were skipped entirely).

### 3.4 "Locally coherent, globally surprising," decomposed by scale rather than by knob

The diffusion-over-centroids plan puts both properties on one dial (cfg_scale). A more principled decomposition assigns each property to the scale that owns it: **local coherence** = writer prefix-conditioning + FiLM (3.2, guaranteed by the token LM); **arc-level surprise** = the sampled concept channel (vMF/MDN/diffusion — sampled, measured against the calibrated novelty reference); **move-level structure** = the delta-transport operator family (strategy J; the one strategy whose novelty is *semantically legible* — "this topic taken in that direction"). The composed stack (J picks the move, the density head samples within it, the writer renders with textual context) has independent, separately-measurable dials at every level — a cleaner instrument than one scalar CFG dial, and each stage exists or is cheap. This endorses the plans' probe C3 and rejection-sampling K, and gives them a home.

### 3.5 Encoder repair belongs on the critical path, not the fallback branch

The brainstorm proved the sentence geometry defect is a *manufactured* one (uniformity/covariance repulsion applied to in-window neighbors; adjacent 0.037 < within-doc-shuffled 0.146) with a ~40-line fix (document-disjoint / neighbor-masked anti-collapse). The fixes plan demotes encoder work to "if everything is mediocre." That sequencing is wrong on cost grounds: every artifact downstream of the encoder — the bank, the writer Phase-2, the reference distribution R, every frontier row — must be recomputed when the encoder changes, and the plan freezes R *immutably* in Step B. Either (a) run the A1/A2 encoder retrain in parallel *now* (it is hours, gated on the four-cell M1 table, and MiniLM has already shown the ceiling is real), decide, and only then freeze R; or (b) explicitly accept the damaged geometry for this entire program and record that all novelty results are conditional on it. Doing Step B's freeze first, encoder later, guarantees paying for the whole eval suite twice.

## 4. Part 3 — The novelty problem: the minimal correct architecture

**What the writer already gives you:** token-level sampling novelty = paraphrase novelty. Necessary, insufficient — different words, same thought.

**What "a thought never written" requires operationally:** a sampled latent driver whose samples are (i) conditioned on context, (ii) on-manifold enough to render, (iii) verifiably distant from the corpus *at the semantic level*, with a calibrated reference for "distant." The strategy doc's §7.2 self-calibrating novelty_rate is the right instrument; it has two gaps:

- **A paraphrase control is missing.** Compute n(x) for *paraphrases* of bank paragraphs (cheap versions: sentence-shuffled paragraphs, or back-translated/LLM-paraphrased if allowed). This locates "same thought, new words" on the novelty axis. A generator whose novelty histogram overlaps the paraphrase band is producing restatements; the held-out band (real new thoughts) is the target. Without this control, the flagship metric cannot distinguish the two things the user most cares about distinguishing.
- **A frozen external encoder should co-measure novelty** (MiniLM, per brainstorm M5): measuring novelty only in the space that defines the generator is circular if that space's geometry is pathological — and this space's geometry is *known* to be pathological.

**Minimal correct architecture (my recommendation, concrete):**
1. Corpus with real documents (A1). 2. Writer retrained with same-sentence conditioning + textual prefix + all-block FiLM (D1–D4, 3.2). 3. A conditional density head over paragraph concepts on the existing `ContextTransformer` trunk — **vMF first, MDN second, diffusion third**, all under the same harness with the same controls; diffusion only after C1/C2 fixes and only if it beats the mixture baseline. 4. Delta-transport (J) and rejection re-ranking (K) as composable, separately-gated add-ons. 5. Novelty defended with: calibrated novelty_rate vs held-out R **plus** paraphrase control **plus** external-encoder n(x) **plus** the shuffled-context conditioning control; multimodality demonstrated via the MDN's weight entropy and the mean-of-8 vs best-of-8 M3 gap (report that gap explicitly — it *is* the multimodality signal).

"Predict a centroid then sample around it" is not wrong, but it is the k=1 special case of the mixture view, and the current plan's version of it (diffusion as coded) degenerates to exactly that (Part 1 C1). The sounder frame is: *learn the conditional distribution at the paragraph scale with the cheapest model that demonstrates multimodality, and let the writer — the component that actually works — do the words, with the concept channel as the novelty/steering input.*

## 6. Part 5 — Strategic verdict (blunt)

**Is "retrain/scale reasoner + diffusion prior at paragraph scale" the right use of compute?** As sequenced in the two plans — **no, not yet.** The direction (stop retrieving; model the conditional distribution; sample; measure a calibrated frontier) is correct and the anti-mirage eval design is genuinely good. But the program as written spends its GPU budget on a flagship experiment that is currently rigged to fail-uninterpretably in three independent ways: the diffusion engine as coded will behave like the mean-seeking regressor the strategy was chosen to escape (Part 1 C1–C2); the text-level claims run through a writer trained on a different map than the one it is used for (D1); and the data cannot contain the phenomenon being sought, because document structure was destroyed before any model ever saw it (A1) — plus the Step A scale-up, as specified, silently swaps the task for near-copy prediction (B1). None of these is expensive to fix. All of them are cheaper than one wasted frontier sweep.

**The categorical judgment:** there is no categorically better *direction* on the table — generation-in-concept-space at a coarse timescale, rendered by the token-level writer, is the right architecture family, and the measured history (sentence-scale ≈ 0, paragraph-scale > 0, writer works) supports it. The corrections are about *which* concrete instantiation and *in what order*:

1. **Fix the substrate first (days, mostly CPU + one encoding pass):** corpus with real documents; writer retrained on the correct mapping with textual-prefix conditioning; diffusion scaling fixed; stride margins specified. Everything in MUST above.
2. **Climb the generative ladder from the bottom (a week):** vMF → MDN → diffusion, same harness, same controls, promotion only on beating the previous rung's frontier. The MDN's mixture weights are the first direct test of the multimodality premise underlying the whole Strategy B argument.
3. **Keep the probes** (σ-sweep with the extra retrieved-anchor arm, blend baseline, delta-clusters) exactly as the fixes plan orders them — they are cheap and well-designed.
4. **Run the encoder repair in parallel, decide, then freeze R.** Freezing the reference distribution before deciding on the encoder is the one sequencing error in the fixes plan that guarantees double-paying for the eval suite.
5. **Place the novelty claim at the arc level**, defended by the calibrated novelty_rate *plus* the paraphrase control *plus* an external encoder — because with a token-sampling writer in the loop, "novel vector statistics" without a paraphrase band cannot distinguish a new thought from a reworded one, and that distinction is the entire point of the project.

The honest one-line version: **the plans fix the measurement; this review says the instrument, the data, and the decoder each have one load-bearing defect the plans froze in place — fix those three, insert the cheap density baselines, and *then* the diffusion bet is worth its GPU hours.**
