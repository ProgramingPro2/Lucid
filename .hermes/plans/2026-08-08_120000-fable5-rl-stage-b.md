## 0. The headline answer first (reward composition: hybrid, and why)

**Is an LLM judge less gameable than the frozen JEPA space? No — it is *differently* gameable, and
for the axis we care most about (does the text track the commanded z) it is strictly MORE gameable.**
The decision, defended:

- The frozen JEPA re-encode cos(E(x_hat), z_cmd) is the *definition* of the deliverable
  (fed_vs_concept is a G1 gate). It cannot co-adapt (frozen sensor, the novelty plan's whole
  calibration argument, §2.3 item 2). Its known exploit is exactly one: **keyword stuffing / topic
  word salad** — degenerate text that lands near z without being a paragraph. That exploit is (a)
  bounded, because cosine saturates (human ceiling 0.65; consecutive-paragraph band 0.705), and (b)
  trivially detectable by surface statistics (distinct-2, n-gram repetition) and by any fluency
  sensor.
- The LLM judge is the only cheap sensor for **fluency / naturalness / discourse coherence**, which
  the JEPA space is blind to (it is a topic codec, 44% teacher-forced recon — it cannot see grammar).
  Its known exploits are the RLHF classics: verbosity bias, list/format bias, confident-tone bias,
  self-preference, score compression. None of these move cos(E(x_hat), z_cmd).
- **The two sensors' failure modes are disjoint and mutually detecting.** Text that games the judge
  (fluent, verbose, generic) scores low on the JEPA term; text that games the JEPA term (keyword
  salad) scores low on the judge and trips the hard collapse gate. That orthogonality is the actual
  argument for the hybrid — not "both are nice", but "each one is the anti-gaming guard for the
  other". A judge-only reward would let the policy drift into fluent z-ignoring (the render-wall
  failure all over again, now paid for with RL compute). A JEPA-only reward re-runs the scratch
  writer's collapse in slow motion.

**Adopted reward (full formula in §3):**

    R(x | z_cmd) = gate(x) * [ w_z * f( cos(E(x_hat), z_cmd) ) + w_j * J_coh(x) ]
    w_z = 0.6, w_j = 0.4
    gate(x) in {0, 1}: hard rule-based collapse/degeneration gate (§3.2)
    f: piecewise-linear ramp saturating at the human topical ceiling (§3.1)
    J_coh: judge coherence score in [0, 1], within-group standardized by GRPO (§1)

The JEPA leg gets the larger weight because controllability is the project's claim and the judge leg
is a guard + polish signal, not the objective. The judge's topic-drift axis is used at EVAL only
(cross-check), never in the training reward — topic is the JEPA space's job; asking a 1.7B judge to
re-derive it from a keyword list would inject a noisier, more gameable copy of a signal we already
measure exactly.

## 1. Reward design: the judge

### 1.1 Model pick

**Train-time judge: `Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B-Instruct` (or the current Qwen3 ~1.7B instruct SKU), 4-bit NF4,
~1.4 GB VRAM.** Justification:

- Best instruction-following-per-GB in the 1-2B class in our experience; reliably emits a single
  digit under a constrained rubric, which is all we ask of it (§1.3: we read score-token logits, we
  do not sample).
- Same tokenizer/loader stack as the policy backbone (already in the HF cache from Phase-A; zero new
  infra; `_resolve_backbone`-style 4-bit load reuses the exact BitsAndBytes config in
  `configs/pivot_step1.json`).
- Self-preference risk (judge and policy share a family) is real but *only matters on axes the judge
  controls*. Here the judge controls only fluency/coherence; topic tracking is JEPA's. Residual risk
  is covered by the held-out judge below.
- Rejected: SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct (weaker rubric compliance at this size), Gemma-2-2B-it as the
  train judge (bigger, slower, and we want it *unused in training* — see next line), a trained tiny
  reward model (no preference data to train it on; 2-day budget forbids building one).

**Held-out eval judge: `google/gemma-2-2b-it`, 4-bit, loaded only during eval passes (never
concurrent with training; swap in/out, ~1.8 GB).** Different family, different pretraining mix,
different biases — a policy that has learned Qwen-judge-specific tics will not transfer them.

### 1.2 Absolute scoring vs k-way ranking: pick absolute-with-group-standardization

The contrastive/rank formulation ("judge ranks k completions for the same z") is right in principle
— rank kills judge scale-bias. But asking a 1-2B model to hold k=8 paragraphs in context and emit a
consistent total order is asking for positional-bias soup; small judges are far more reliable on
one-paragraph-at-a-time rubric scoring. The resolution: **score each sample absolutely (cheap,
cacheable, order-free), then let GRPO's group-relative advantage — (r_i - mean_group) / std_group —
do the ranking.** Within a group all k samples share the same z and the same judge, so any monotone
scale bias cancels exactly; this IS the rank formulation, computed robustly. Optional tie-break
(OFF by default): if within-group judge std < 0.15, run round-robin pairwise A/B prompts on the top
half; only enable if score compression is observed (§6 failure modes).

### 1.3 Scoring mechanics: logit-read, not generation

One forward pass per (rubric, sample); read the next-token logits restricted to tokens "1".."5";
score = softmax-expected value, rescaled to [0,1] via (E[s]-1)/4. No sampling noise, no parsing, no
refusals, ~32 samples scored in a couple of seconds. Prompt order randomized where lists appear.

### 1.4 Literal prompt templates

Judge axis A — coherence/fluency (THE train-time judge signal, J_coh):

```
<|im_start|>system
You are a strict writing evaluator. You judge one paragraph at a time.
Ignore the paragraph's topic and length. Judge ONLY: grammar, fluency,
sentence-to-sentence flow, and whether it reads as sane, natural prose a
competent human could have written. Long does not mean good. Lists,
headers, or fragments are not paragraphs. Answer with a single digit.
<|im_end|>
<|im_start|>user
PARAGRAPH:
{generated_paragraph}

Score the paragraph:
1 = broken (ungrammatical, incoherent, or degenerate/repetitive)
2 = poor (readable but disjointed or unnatural)
3 = acceptable (minor awkwardness)
4 = good (fluent, coherent)
5 = excellent (indistinguishable from a well-edited human paragraph)
Answer with one digit only.
<|im_end|>
<|im_start|>assistant
```

Judge axis B — topic adherence (EVAL-ONLY cross-check; commanded z rendered as text via its 5
nearest zbank paragraphs' TF-IDF keywords, top 12 terms):

```
<|im_start|>system
You are a strict topical evaluator. Answer with a single digit.
<|im_end|>
<|im_start|>user
TARGET TOPIC (keywords): {kw_1}, {kw_2}, ..., {kw_12}

PARAGRAPH:
{generated_paragraph}

Did the paragraph stay on the target topic?
1 = unrelated  2 = tangential  3 = partially on topic
4 = clearly on topic  5 = fully and specifically on topic
Being long or well-written does NOT count as on-topic.
Answer with one digit only.
<|im_end|>
<|im_start|>assistant
```

Judge axis C — degeneration (EVAL-ONLY redundancy check on the hard gate; same shape: "1 = heavy
repetition/empty/looping ... 5 = no degeneration"). At train time degeneration is handled by the
deterministic gate (§3.2), which is faster and non-gameable.

**Aggregation at train time:** J_coh (axis A) only, in [0,1], entering R with weight w_j = 0.4;
group standardization by GRPO. Axes B/C run every eval checkpoint on the fixed probe set, on BOTH
judges, and feed the anti-gaming decision rule (§4), not the gradient.

## 2. RL algorithm: GRPO, with a mandatory cheap phase before it

### 2.1 Pick: GRPO (group-relative, no critic). Rejections first

- **PPO: rejected.** A learned value head is a fourth network on an 11 GB card, is the classic
  instability source at small scale, and its whole purpose (variance reduction) is delivered free by
  group-relative baselines when k samples share a prompt. Nothing PPO adds is worth the VRAM.
- **DPO-from-sampled-preferences: rejected as the main method, absorbed as the fallback.** DPO on
  (best, worst)-of-k pairs scored by our reward is workable offline, but it optimizes a proxy of a
  proxy (Bradley-Terry fit to a scalar we already have), discards the k-2 middle samples, and its
  implicit reward drifts from ours. If GRPO proves unstable on this rig (§6), DPO on the already-
  collected rollouts is the salvage path — the data is reusable, which is why rollout logs are kept
  in full (§5.4).
- **GRPO: adopted.** Matches the rank/contrastive reward formulation natively (§1.2), needs only the
  policy + a frozen reference, and is proven at exactly this recipe (grouped rollouts, standardized
  advantages, clipped ratio, explicit KL to a reference).

### 2.2 Phase B0 first: RAFT / best-of-k distillation (the "small P-step" — answered explicitly)

**Could reward-weighted MLE / rejection sampling get most of the benefit cheaper? Partially — and it
is strictly dominant to run it FIRST, so it is mandatory, not optional.** Best-of-k distillation
(RAFT): sample k=8 per prompt at the RL generation params, keep the argmax-R sample per prompt if it
clears an acceptance bar, one epoch of CE on winners (the exact Stage-A trainer loop, new dataset).
Cost/benefit vs GRPO:

- Cost: ~4k prompts x 8 rollouts = 32k generations (~5 h) + 1 CE epoch on ~3k winners (~1.5 h) +
  eval (~1 h). Total ~7-8 h, all machinery already exists (`train_stage_a.py` retargeted at a
  winners JSONL; the rollout loop is `eval_sample_log` batched).
- Benefit: RAFT reliably captures the "move probability mass onto the good mode you can already
  produce" gains — typically 50-70% of what RL delivers when the base policy already samples good
  outputs at rate >= ~1/8 (which G1-passing implies for the JEPA term). It cannot push the frontier
  (no signal from losers, no KL-controlled exploration).
- Verdict: run B0, evaluate against the Stage-B gates (§5). If B0 alone clears them — done, report,
  and GRPO becomes optional polish. If not, B0's checkpoint is a strictly better GRPO init and its
  rollouts calibrate the reward statistics (group std, gate trip rate) before any RL step is spent.
  Acceptance bar for winners: gate(x)=1 AND cos(E(x_hat), z_cmd) >= 0.50 AND J_coh >= 0.5.

### 2.3 GRPO hyperparameters (exact)

| Knob | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Policy init | Phase-A (or B0) checkpoint: adapter + LoRA r=16, alpha=32, backbone 4-bit NF4 bf16-compute | unchanged trainable set (~17M adapter + ~9M LoRA) |
| Reference (KL anchor) | frozen copy of Phase-A adapter weights + Phase-A LoRA loaded as a second, disabled-for-training peft adapter; ref logprobs via adapter switch | NOT the raw base model — the anchor is the gate-passing actuator |
| Group size k | 8 | matches B0, matches best-of-8 critic machinery in the novelty plan |
| Prompts per step | 4 (=> 32 rollouts/optimizer step) | |
| Rollout length | max_new_tokens 200, min_new_tokens 40 | paragraph scale; matches max_target_tokens 256 regime |
| Generation | temperature 0.9, top_p 0.95, no repetition penalty (the gate must see honest degeneration) | |
| Advantage | A_i = (r_i - mean_k) / (std_k + 1e-4), per group | |
| Loss | token-level clipped ratio, length-normalized per sequence (divide each sample's token sum by its own length — kills the verbosity gradient) | |
| Clip epsilon | 0.2 | |
| KL penalty | beta = 0.05, per-token k3 estimator vs reference, added to loss; controller: if mean KL > 12 nats/paragraph, beta *= 1.5; if < 2, beta *= 0.7 (checked every 25 steps) | |
| PPO epochs | 1 (strictly on-policy, no sample reuse) | stability > sample efficiency at this scale |
| LR | adapter 2e-5, LoRA 5e-6, AdamW wd 0.0, cosine to 20%, warmup 20 steps | ~50x below SFT LRs — RL moves logits, not representations |
| Grad clip | 1.0 | as Stage-A |
| Micro-batch | 4 sequences backward at a time, grad-accum 8 | fits activations at 200-token rollouts |
| Total steps | 600 planned (~19k rollouts), checkpoints every 50, anti-gaming battery every 100 | |
| Entropy | no bonus; monitor. If mean token entropy falls > 40% from init, halve LR once; if again, stop | |

**VRAM budget (fits 10-11 GB):** 4-bit backbone ~1.5 GB + LoRA/adapter + AdamW states (fp32, ~26M
params -> ~0.4 GB) + frozen ref adapter copies (~0.1 GB) + train judge 4-bit ~1.4 GB (resident) +
KV cache for batch-8 x ~460-token sequences ~0.7 GB + activations at micro-batch 4 ~2-3 GB + CUDA
overhead ~1 GB => ~7.5-8.5 GB peak. JEPA codec stays on CPU exactly as in `train_stage_a.py`
(`JepaCodec(..., device="cpu")`); MiniLM eval on CPU. Keep `expandable_segments:True`. GpuGate wraps
the loop as in Stage-A (lemonade daemon coexistence). If the daemon spikes and 8.5 GB is not there:
first drop the resident judge to swap-in-per-step (adds ~15 s/step), then k=6.

## 3. The reward formula and its gradient behavior

### 3.1 Full formula

    c_i     = cos( E(x_hat_i), z_cmd )                      # frozen JEPA re-encode, CPU
    f(c)    = clip( (c - 0.35) / (0.62 - 0.35), 0, 1 )      # ramp: floor 0.35, SATURATES at 0.62
    J_i     = (E[judge digit] - 1) / 4                      # axis A logit-read, in [0,1]
    gate_i  = 0 if any collapse rule trips (below) else 1
    r_i     = gate_i * ( 0.6 * f(c_i) + 0.4 * J_i )
    A_i     = (r_i - mean_k(r)) / (std_k(r) + 1e-4)         # GRPO group standardization

The saturation point 0.62 sits just below the human MiniLM ceiling (0.65) and well below the
consecutive-paragraph JEPA band (0.705): **once the text is as on-topic as human text is, more
topical density earns zero additional reward.** This single clip is the main structural defense
against keyword stuffing — the gradient through the JEPA leg vanishes exactly where stuffing starts
paying.

### 3.2 Hard collapse gate (deterministic, non-gameable, computed in Python)

gate = 0 if ANY of: token length < 40 after strip; distinct-2 < 0.35; any 4-gram occurs >= 4 times;
longest verbatim loop >= 30 tokens repeated; non-ASCII/control-char fraction > 20%; output is
list-formatted (>= 3 lines starting with bullets/numbers). A gated sample contributes r_i = 0 and
therefore a strongly negative standardized advantage within its group — degeneration is punished by
its own groupmates, no judge call wasted (skip judging gated samples).

### 3.3 Why each named shortcut loses

- **Verbosity:** three independent blocks. The judge system prompt says long is not better; the
  per-sequence length normalization removes the "more tokens, more reward-bearing gradient mass"
  artifact that makes verbosity a *gradient* attractor even under length-neutral rewards; hard cap
  200 new tokens. The JEPA leg is mean-pooled and length-invariant.
- **Repetition/looping:** gate -> r = 0 -> within-group advantage ~ -std below mean. This is the
  strongest negative signal in the system and it is unlearnable-around (rule-based, not a model).
- **Off-topic-but-fluent-and-verbose:** the judge leg maxes at 0.4 * 1.0 = 0.4; a groupmate that is
  merely acceptable prose (J = 0.5) but on-topic (f = 1) earns 0.8. Under group standardization the
  off-topic sample sits below group mean whenever ANY sibling tracks z — and G1-passing guarantees
  siblings track z at high rate. The gradient consistently points toward the topic.
- **z-ignoring (one generic paragraph for every command):** cannot score f above ~ramp(0.45) on
  average, because bank commands are spread at cross-doc cos ~0.57 — one output cannot be near all
  of them. Additionally the across-prompt self-BLEU guard (§4) catches the residual "small menu of
  generic paragraphs" variant that per-group rewards cannot see.
- **Judge-tic exploitation (Qwen-pleasing phrasing):** worth at most 0.4, capped, and detected by
  the held-out Gemma judge divergence check (§4).

## 4. Anti-gaming / anti-mirage protocol (the decision rule, with numbers)

**Frozen before step 0:** probe set P = 128 held-out bank z (doc-disjoint, the `held` split of
`ParaPairDataset`) + 32 MDN-sampled novel z, with fixed contexts and fixed decode params (greedy,
as `eval_sample_log`). Phase-A baseline numbers on P are measured once and written to
`artifacts/pivot_stageB/baseline_phaseA.json` before any Stage-B training.

**Battery, every 100 GRPO steps (and once after B0), ~20 min each:**

| Signal | Instrument | Green | Red |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tracking (the real objective) | median cos(E(x_hat), z_cmd) on P | >= baseline | < baseline - 0.02 twice consecutively |
| Renderability | median MiniLM writer-fidelity on P | >= baseline - 0.02 | below that twice |
| Train-judge coherence | axis A, Qwen judge, mean on P | rising or flat | (cannot be red alone) |
| Held-out-judge coherence | axis A, Gemma judge, mean on P | tracks train judge within 0.08 | train judge - heldout judge gap > 0.15 |
| Diversity | distinct-2 per sample (mean); self-BLEU-4 across the 128 P outputs | distinct-2 >= 0.45; self-BLEU <= baseline + 0.08 | either violated twice |
| Length | mean new tokens | within +/- 40% of baseline | > +60% |
| KL leash | mean KL to Phase-A ref | 2-12 nats/paragraph | > 20 sustained |

**Decision rule (pre-registered):**

- PROCEED if tracking is green AND at least one of {held-out judge, renderability} improved AND no
  red anywhere.
- **REWARD-HACKING DETECTED — STOP, revert to last green checkpoint, write the incident report —
  if:** train reward increased >= 0.05 over the window while (tracking red) OR (train/heldout judge
  gap red) OR (diversity red). This is exactly the user's specified hard stop: judge-score up with
  fed_vs_concept flat-or-down is the signature, and the frozen JEPA sensor is the arbiter because
  it is the one instrument the optimization cannot touch.
- STALL (neither) for 300 consecutive steps: stop, keep best checkpoint, report honestly that GRPO
  plateaued.

The 20-seed confirm discipline applies to the FINAL claim only (§5): final gate numbers are
re-measured with 20 decode seeds on P before any "pass" is declared. No mid-run number is quotable.

## 5. Data, prompts, rollouts, logging

### 5.1 Prompt construction (reuses Phase-A data exactly)

A Stage-B prompt is (z_cmd, context):

- 50% — held-out-split bank z with its true previous paragraph as context (the Stage-A `held` +
  unused `train` tail via `ParaPairDataset`; z from `artifacts/corpus_v2/zbank.npz`).
- 25% — fiber-perturbed bank z: z_tilde ~ vMF(z_bank, kappa=700) via `fiber_corrupt` (train-test
  command-distribution match, same contract as Stage-A).
- 25% — MDN-sampled novel destinations (`MixtureVmfHead` / `sample_vmf` from
  `experiments/sentence_pred/heads.py`), context = the bank paragraph nearest the sampled z. This
  slice is what makes Stage-B practice the actual downstream task (novel destinations, N2).

Input layout identical to Stage-A inference: `[soft_prefix(z); tokenize(prev, max 256)]` via
`SoftPrefixAdapter` + `build_inputs_embeds`. No prefix-drop at RL time.

### 5.2 Generation params

Rollouts: temperature 0.9, top_p 0.95, max_new_tokens 200, min_new_tokens 40, batch = one group of
8 sharing the KV-cached prompt. Eval on P: greedy, max 128 — matching `eval_sample_log` so numbers
are comparable to Phase-A logs.

### 5.3 Rollout budget vs 2 days

Throughput estimate (4-bit 1.7B, HF generate, batch 8): ~35-50 s per 32-rollout step for
generation + ~3 s judging (32 logit reads) + ~4 s JEPA CPU encode + ~25-35 s backward/step =>
**~1.5-2.0 min/step, ~30-40 steps/h.** 600 steps = 15-20 h of GRPO. B0 = 32k generations ~5 h +
distill ~1.5 h. Total planned GPU time ~26-30 h out of 48 — honest headroom for one restart, the
eval batteries, and daemon contention.

### 5.4 Logging (feeds the atlas for free)

Every rollout row: (z_cmd, source-of-z, ctx id, text, c_i, J_i, gate flags, length, KL, step) ->
`artifacts/pivot_stageB/rollouts.jsonl.zst`. This is simultaneously the DPO-fallback dataset, the
disturbance-field / gain-map data (novelty plan §2.6d), and the audit trail for the incident report
if the hard stop fires.

## 6. Stage-B gates (pre-registered) and the 2-day schedule

### 6.1 Gates — what "win" means vs the Phase-A baseline (all on P, 20 decode seeds, final only)

| Metric | Phase-A floor (G1) | Stage-B PASS |
|---|---|---|
| fed_vs_concept: median cos(E(x_hat), z_cmd) | 0.55 | >= baseline + 0.03 AND >= 0.58 |
| 2-AFC z-identification (bank-typical pair sep ~0.57) | 85% | >= baseline AND >= 88% |
| Held-out (Gemma) judge coherence, mean axis A | measured baseline | >= baseline + 0.08 (on the [0,1] scale), i.e. ~ +0.3 digits |
| MiniLM renderability median | 0.40 | >= baseline - 0.02 (must-not-regress) |
| Fluency non-regression | pairwise blind vs Phase-A outputs, Gemma judge, position-swapped | Stage-B win-rate >= 55% |
| Diversity | — | distinct-2 >= 0.45; self-BLEU-4 <= baseline + 0.05 |

HARD STOP (restated as a gate): if final train-judge score rose but fed_vs_concept < baseline, the
run is declared reward-hacked regardless of every other number; ship the incident report, not the
checkpoint. Partial credit is pre-registered too: tracking + 2-AFC pass but coherence flat = "RL
added controllability, not polish" — reportable, keep checkpoint, no headline claim on coherence.

### 6.2 Two-day schedule (3090 wall clock)

- **H0-H3:** freeze baselines: run P through Phase-A checkpoint (greedy + 20 seeds), both judges,
  MiniLM, JEPA; write `baseline_phaseA.json`. Build reward module + gate rules + judge logit-reader.
  Smoke-test 5 GRPO steps at k=4 for VRAM/throughput truth.
- **H3-H11:** Phase B0 (RAFT): 4k prompts x 8, score, distill winners 1 epoch via the Stage-A
  trainer pointed at the winners set (LR halved, 1 epoch).
- **H11-H14:** full eval of B0 vs gates. Branch: gates pass -> polish + report, GRPO optional.
- **H14-H36:** GRPO ~600 steps from the B0 checkpoint; battery at 100/200/.../600; KL controller
  and entropy monitor active; checkpoint every 50.
- **H36-H44:** final battery: 20-seed gate numbers, 2-AFC, pairwise fluency, both judges, diversity.
- **H44-H48:** buffer (one restart OR the DPO salvage on logged rollouts) + written report.

### 6.3 Honest expected outcome and failure modes

**Is GRPO on a 1.7B policy on one 3090 in 2 days realistic? Yes, marginally — ~600 steps / ~19k
rollouts is a small but genuine RL run, sufficient for the modest pre-registered deltas (+0.03
tracking, +0.08 judge) precisely because Phase-A already passed G1 and B0 front-loads the easy
gains. It is NOT sufficient for open-ended capability gains, and the spec does not claim any.**
Probability estimates: B0 alone clears the must-not-regress gates and >= half the improvement gates
~55%; GRPO adds enough to clear all gates ~45% conditional on reaching H36 healthy; at least one of
{hard stop, stall} fires ~30%. Named failure modes:

1. **Judge score compression** (everything scores 4): within-group std ~ 0 -> advantages dominated
   by the JEPA leg (acceptable degradation) or noise. Detected at H0-H3 smoke test; mitigation:
   temperature 1.5 on the score-token softmax, then the pairwise tie-break of §1.2.
2. **Generation throughput miss** (HF generate slower than estimated under daemon contention):
   cut to 400 steps and k=6 before cutting anything else; the gates were sized for small deltas.
3. **KL cliff:** too-tight beta = frozen policy (stall rule catches it); too-loose = drift into
   judge-pleasing register (battery catches it). The beta controller bounds both.
4. **Entropy collapse to a house style:** self-BLEU guard is the tripwire; LR halving is the brake.
5. **The interesting failure:** tracking improves but held-out judge falls — the policy trades
   fluency for topical precision. That is a real trade-off finding, not a bug; report it with the
   frontier plot from the battery logs.
